# [L] Authored tracks default to a 30 s max-age, with explicit unlimited

## Goal

A newly authored track, raw or media, advertises a 30 s publisher max-age unless its author chooses otherwise, in Rust, JS, FFI, C, and every hand-written binding. Unlimited stays expressible explicitly in each of them. Received metadata keeps its absence semantics, the relay keeps its unset (no) local age ceiling, and no wire value or wire absence changes meaning.

## Plan

Decided 2026-10-30 (maintainer): audit before deciding whether max-age should default to 0 instead of unlimited, following the subscriber max-delay decision in [moq-dev/moq#7403](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/pull/7403). The audit landed in [moq-dev/moq#7567](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/pull/7567), and the maintainer settled its three proposals:

- Decided 2026-11-01 (maintainer): ✅ **30 s for both raw and media authored retention**, over 0 raw with 30 s media, or keeping raw unlimited. Thirty seconds reuses the existing media policy and exceeds the recorder's default 10 s reorder allowance without making live viewers wait; zero would drop superseded-group overlap and past fetch.
- Decided 2026-11-01 (maintainer): ✅ **the relay keeps its current unset ceiling**, over defaulting relay `duration` to 30 s with an operator opt-out. A finite relay ceiling would restrict explicit long-DVR and archive publishers too; the existing 30 s idle expiry is a different policy and stays as is.
- Decided 2026-11-01 (maintainer): ✅ **the authoring API applies the default at construction, with an explicit unlimited and unchanged decoded metadata**, over a new default/unlimited/finite retention type across every authoring API. Changing `Info::default` / `infoDefaults` alone is ruled out: it would silently give incoming tracks 30 s and break explicit unlimited through the current conversions.

The audit below is the implementation's map of every surface; the Implementation section at the end is the work.

### Current defaults

Publisher max-age is a freshness bound on **superseded groups**. A group expires when either its successor's arrival is old enough at this hop or the successor's first timestamp trails the live edge enough. Untimed tracks use the wall clock. The newest group is exempt. Byte reclamation has a separate cache policy. See [rs/moq-net/src/model/track.rs:120](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/model/track.rs#L120) and [js/net/src/track.ts:137](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/js/net/src/track.ts#L137).

### Subscriber and wire absence are separate

- Rust subscriptions default to `max_delay = Some(0)`; JS omitted subscriber `maxDelay` means 0. Explicit Rust `None` / JS `null` uses publisher retention alone. Changing publisher retention is not another latency-default change. See [rs/moq-net/src/model/subscription.rs:23](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/model/subscription.rs#L23) and [js/net/src/track.ts:233](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/js/net/src/track.ts#L233).
- A direct fetch has its own options, defaulting to no *subscriber* cap. It still obeys publisher/local retention: Rust `Fetching::poll` clamps the budget and returns `Error::Old` before serving stale data. A more tolerant subscriber cannot recover history expired by publisher policy. See [rs/moq-net/src/model/group.rs:4112](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/model/group.rs#L4112) and [rs/moq-net/src/model/track.rs:5531](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/model/track.rs#L5531).
- For an IETF SUBSCRIBE with absent MAX_AGE, Rust's `serving_subscription` sets `max_delay = None`; JS uses its largest safe integer budget. This means no additional subscriber restriction, **not guaranteed unlimited storage**. Explicit MAX_AGE overrides this. See [rs/moq-net/src/ietf/publisher.rs:30](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/ietf/publisher.rs#L30), [rs/moq-net/src/ietf/publisher.rs:1932](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/ietf/publisher.rs#L1932), and [js/net/src/ietf/publisher.ts:504](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/js/net/src/ietf/publisher.ts#L504).
- The checkout's Timestamp extension says SUBSCRIBE absence preserves publisher delivery policy and update absence preserves the current budget; zero expires superseded groups, never the newest. This is extension semantics, not a base-IETF default of zero: [drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-timestamp.md:142](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-timestamp.md#L142). The published [timestamp-01](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-lcurley-moq-timestamp-01.html) predates this MAX_AGE section; do not cite that revision as specifying it.
- Base [moq-transport-22 section 10.3](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-moq-transport-22.html#section-10.3) defines a different property, MAX_CACHE_DURATION: absence allows caching until implementation constraints evict objects. Its present-value rule measures age from receipt of each object, unlike this model's superseded-group freshness. The implementation maps publisher age to that property ([rs/moq-net/src/ietf/properties.rs:89](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/blob/c95a9b1201/rs/moq-net/src/ietf/properties.rs#L89)). That pre-existing distinction is not resolved by the authored default. No wire omission is reinterpreted by this quest.

### Concrete consequences

For a live feed with successive groups A then B, **zero publisher age makes A stale as soon as B supersedes it**, even if A's payload remains allocated until cache reclamation. A late join can still read the newest retained B from its start, including its keyframe or snapshot. Zero does not inherently break every late join. It removes overlap for catch-up, switching/warmup that needs A, and direct fetching of A once superseded. Even with zero, the newest group can be large or old in absolute time.

With **30 s publisher age**, a reader can request superseded groups within the age window, provided they remain cached or can be filled. That does not delay a default live reader, whose own budget is still zero. The window is measured at group reach, not an exact 30 s of first-frame history, and idle expiry, byte pressure, upstream availability, track lifetime, and decoder needs can shorten useful history. This is eligibility, not a guaranteed DVR service level.

HLS/DASH needs past media for listed segments and players starting several target durations behind live. Zero media retention makes those segment fetches fail once their media groups are superseded. A finite value must cover the advertised playlist and fetch/reorder needs; 30 s is the existing media policy, not a proof that every possible playlist configuration fits. The HLS record track is different: its newest group restates its window.

DVR and archive need two policies: enough live overlap to record reordered groups without gaps, and durable storage retention to seek later. Zero source age can lose superseded content before recording or eliminate reorder tolerance; it does not erase bytes already stored. Thirty seconds is not a long DVR. Archive replay deliberately advertises no publisher limit, so historical reads remain possible while the store retains them; a relay's local ceiling may still restrict them. Keep this intentional exception explicit.

### Implementation

One shared 30 s constant serves raw and media; `hang::container::MAX_AGE` reuses it rather than keeping a second value. The default applies where an author builds a track, never where a peer's metadata is decoded.

- **Rust raw:** an authored `track::Info` gets `max_age: Some(30 s)` unless the author sets it; explicit `None` stays unlimited. Every decode and republish path (lite, IETF, archive playback) states the received value explicitly, so wire absence still yields `None`; audit `..Default::default()` in those paths before changing any default.
- **Rust media:** follows decision 3, so mux `Config.max_age` and `with_max_age` stop meaning "inherit": omitted is 30 s, `None` is unlimited, and a value is that value. This changes the meaning of today's `with_max_age(None)`, so the PR is breaking and says so. CLI `--max-age` unset and the RTMP/SRT/RTC overrides stay at 30 s.
- **Catalog, MSF catalog, and HLS record tracks** inherit the raw default and become 30 s. Their newest group bootstraps a join, so this is harmless; switching them to zero is not part of this quest.
- **Explicit policies stay:** GStreamer's 5 s opaque data, the JS catalog's `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, room snapshots' one day, and archive playback's explicit no-limit republish.
- **JS:** split authoring options from decoded `Info`. At producer construction an omitted or `undefined` `maxAge` is 30,000 ms, `null` is unlimited (matching [moq-dev/moq#7403](https://github.com/moq-dev/moq/pull/7403)'s `null` max-delay), and a number is that policy. `infoDefaults` and decoded `Info` keep absence as no publisher limit. `@moq/hang`'s `trackInfo` and `@moq/publish` gain the same `null`.
- **UniFFI and bindings:** fix the conversions that would otherwise inherit the new Rust default for explicit unlimited: UniFFI's `if let Some` in `rs/moq-ffi/src/producer.rs` and C's `if max_age_present` in `rs/moq-c/src/api.rs`. Where omission and unlimited can't be told apart (a UniFFI record default, a Go nil, a zeroed C struct), omitted means 30 s and an explicit unlimited flag carries unlimited, following the pattern [Subscriptions take a Delay](/quest/m1/subscription-delay.md) sets for max-delay. Python, Swift, Kotlin, Go, Dart, and C++ wrappers expose the same three cases.
- **Relay:** no change. Unset `duration` keeps no local age ceiling and its 30 s idle expiry; byte budgets and cache expiry are untouched.
- **Migration:** raw publishers relying on unlimited history must request unlimited, or a longer window, at construction, before publishing a new track or broadcast identity. Readers asking for history still set a sufficient max-delay and range. The PR title carries `!`, its Impact bullet says how to migrate, migration notes go under `## Unreleased`, and the Rust, JS, binding, and user docs change together.
- **Tests:** cover authored omission versus explicit unlimited in each binding, decoded absence staying unlimited, timed and untimed supersession at 30 s, a late join to an unchanged newest snapshot, a direct fetch of a superseded group inside and past the window, media segment fetch, and archive replay. Use mocked time and the existing harnesses, and run all-language interop for the API and FFI scope. Re-check the overlapping PRs listed above rather than duplicating their fixes.

Public API: breaking in Rust, JS, and every binding (the authored default, and mux `with_max_age(None)`). Wire: none; only authored advertisements change.
